# Exact two-graph transport makes the preparation problem finite-jet

## 1. Result (L289, 2026-07-26)

Let \(H\) and \(\widetilde H\) be Hermitian matrices or formal
Hermitian series, split relative to a fixed endpoint \(F\) and its
complement \(P=I-F\).  Assume both interior blocks are invertible.
Let

\[
{\cal U}(H)=FHF-FHP(PHP)^{-1}PHF
\]

be the endpoint Schur complement, and define its graph column

\[
\boxed{
J_H=F-P(PHP)^{-1}PHF.}                            \tag{1}
\]

Here \(F\) in (1) denotes the endpoint inclusion on its domain.
Then the complete nonlinear endpoint difference has the exact
bilinear form

\[
\boxed{
{\cal U}(\widetilde H)-{\cal U}(H)
=J_{\widetilde H}^*(\widetilde H-H)J_H.}          \tag{2}
\]

The adjoint-looking orientation is equal to the same Hermitian
difference:

\[
\boxed{
{\cal U}(\widetilde H)-{\cal U}(H)
=J_H^*(\widetilde H-H)J_{\widetilde H}.}          \tag{3}
\]

Thus every nonlinear endpoint cross generated by transporting a
preparation gauge is already contained in one mixed graph pairing.
There is no need to derive separate Schur-square formulas at every
grade.

For a fixed repeated Crabb length \(L\), the preparation campaign is
also a **finite-jet** problem.  If a polynomial free row produces,
through degree \(2L\), a uniform positive finite-jet certificate with
terminal term

\[
\delta c^{2L}B_LB_L^*,\qquad \delta>0,            \tag{4}
\]

then L201's invertibility of \(B_L\) and L194's analytic chart make
the complete endpoint positive for all sufficiently small \(c>0\).
No infinitely summable family of preparation coefficients is needed.

L289 does not construct the arbitrary-grade prepared columns or
prove their positive finite-jet certificate.  It corrects the
architecture and the convergence stop condition for A178:

> prove a finite right-ideal/graph recurrence through grades
> \(1,\ldots,L\), with finite bounds and uniform face margins for each
> fixed \(L\); do not demand an infinite globally summable column
> series.

The two-graph identity is elementary block Gaussian elimination and
is not claimed as a new general Schur-complement theorem.  Its use as
the nonlinear transport coordinate for this campaign is the new
reduction.

## 2. Proof of the mixed graph identity

Write the blocks as

\[
H=
\begin{bmatrix}
A&B\\ B^*&D
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
J_H=
\begin{bmatrix}
I\\-D^{-1}B^*
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

Direct multiplication gives

\[
\boxed{
HJ_H=
\begin{bmatrix}
{\cal U}(H)\\0
\end{bmatrix},\qquad
J_H^*H=
\begin{bmatrix}
{\cal U}(H)&0
\end{bmatrix}.}                                  \tag{5}
\]

Apply (5) once to \(H\) and once to \(\widetilde H\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
J_{\widetilde H}^*
(\widetilde H-H)J_H
&=J_{\widetilde H}^*\widetilde HJ_H
  -J_{\widetilde H}^*HJ_H\\
&={\cal U}(\widetilde H)-{\cal U}(H).
\end{aligned}
\]

This proves (2).  Interchanging the matrices proves (3), or one may
take the adjoint of (2).

No positivity is required; only invertibility of the two interior
blocks.  The same calculation is valid coefficientwise for formal
series because an invertible constant interior coefficient has a
unique formal inverse.

## 3. Triangular coefficient transport

Put

\[
\Delta H=\widetilde H-H,\qquad
J_H=\sum_{j\ge0}c^jJ_j,\qquad
J_{\widetilde H}=\sum_{i\ge0}c^i\widetilde J_i .
\]

Equation (2) gives the exact coefficient formula

\[
\boxed{
[c^n]\Delta{\cal U}
=\sum_{i+d+j=n}\widetilde J_i^*
  (\Delta H_d)J_j.}                               \tag{6}
\]

If \(\Delta H=O(c^r)\), then \(\Delta{\cal U}=O(c^r)\), and the
degree-\(n\) endpoint uses only:

1. the new state coefficient \(\Delta H_n\);
2. earlier state changes \(\Delta H_d\), \(d<n\); and
3. already known graph coefficients.

This is the endpoint counterpart of L285's triangular Stein-factor
recurrence.  It also gives the norm estimate

\[
\boxed{
\|\Delta{\cal U}(c)\|
\le
\|J_{\widetilde H}(c)\|\,
\|\Delta H(c)\|\,
\|J_H(c)\|.}                                      \tag{7}
\]

Near the repeated equality point, the interior upper gap stays
uniformly positive, so both graph columns are uniformly analytic and
bounded.  Ordinary Cauchy estimates then give at-most-exponential
coefficient growth whenever the state transport is analytic.  The
Schur shorting itself creates no additional convergence obstruction.

## 4. L288 as the first nontrivial application

Take \(H\) to be the old prepared upper-gap state series and
\(\widetilde H\) the L288 transported series.  Their difference is
the negative transported metric motion.

At degree six, formula (6) contains:

1. the direct compression of \(Z_6^{\rm tr}\); and
2. the first graph-motion pairing of the degree-four change
   \(-Z_4\) with the unchanged degree-two cross block.

These are exactly the three terms called \(\Omega_6\) in L288:

\[
\Omega_6
=FZ_6^{\rm tr}F
+FM_2J(-Z_4)F+F(-Z_4)JM_2F.
\]

L288 proves that their **sum**, not merely the raw metric corner,
factors as

\[
\Omega_6=A_1(ESF)+(A_1ESF)^*.
\]

Equation (2) explains why that complete factorization is the natural
inductive object.  At later grades the target should be stated as

\[
[c^n]\{J_{\widetilde H}^*\Delta HJ_H\}
\]

lying in the appropriate hereditary transfer ideal.  Expanding and
estimating every Schur-square component separately is both
unnecessary and error-prone.

## 5. Why a finite prepared jet suffices

Fix a repeated block length \(L\) and copy multiplicity \(m\).
Choose a free row that is polynomial in \(c\) through degree \(2L\).
L194 then supplies a complete real-analytic exact metric and hence an
analytic upper endpoint

\[
{\cal U}(T,c)
={\cal U}_{\le2L}(T,c)+R(T,c).
\]

On a sufficiently small compact operator neighbourhood, analyticity
gives a uniform tail bound

\[
\boxed{\|R(T,c)\|\le M c^{2L+1}.}                 \tag{8}
\]

Assume the finite preparation has been organized—by completed
squares or an equivalent Schur-flag certificate—so that

\[
\boxed{
{\cal U}_{\le2L}(T,c)
\succeq\delta c^{2L}B_L(T)B_L(T)^*.}              \tag{9}
\]

L201 gives \(B_L(T_0)\) invertible at the repeated apex.  After
shrinking the operator neighbourhood,

\[
\sigma_{\min}(B_L(T))\ge\beta>0.
\]

Equations (8)--(9) yield

\[
{\cal U}(T,c)
\succeq
c^{2L}\{\delta\beta^2-Mc\}I.                     \tag{10}
\]

Therefore

\[
0<c\le\frac{\delta\beta^2}{2M}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
{\cal U}(T,c)\succeq
\frac{\delta\beta^2}{2}c^{2L}I\succ0.            \tag{11}
\]

Every higher coefficient is absorbed by the terminal positive Gram.
The preparation row itself is a finite polynomial, so no infinite
series of selected columns needs to converge.

This does not remove the hard part: A178 must still construct the
finite jet (9) for arbitrary \(L\), including odd cancellations,
even transported faces, partial-flag Schur costs, and bounds through
rank changes.  It does remove the stronger and unnecessary demand
that the coefficient norms be summable uniformly over infinitely
many grades.
