# Scalar equality splits a classical Gau--Wu disk-model summand

> **Campaign scope.**  This closes L325's terminal zero face for
> fixed Crabb length and copy multiplicity.  The load-bearing
> reducing-summand theorem is Gau--Wu (2009), not a new campaign
> result.  The local application and copy-multiplicity bookkeeping
> are recorded here so the classical input is not silently
> rediscovered or overstated.

## 1. Result (L326, 2026-07-26)

Put \(p=L+1\ge3\) and

\[
 T_0=C_p\otimes I_m.
\]

Shrink to a fixed neighbourhood of \(T_0\).  Let \(T\) be a matrix
in that neighbourhood with \(w(T)\le1\), let
\(f\in H^\infty(\mathbb D)\) satisfy \(\|f\|_\infty\le1\), and
suppose

\[
 \|f(T)x\|=2
 \qquad(\|x\|=1).                                  \tag{1}
\]

Then \(T\) has an orthogonal reducing decomposition

\[
 T\cong A_f\oplus T',
 \qquad
 A_f=X_\phi S(\phi)X_\phi^{-1},
 \qquad
 \phi(z)=zf(z),                                    \tag{2}
\]

where \(f\) is inner, \(f(0)=0\), and

\[
 X_\phi=\sqrt2\,I_{\ker S(\phi)}
 \oplus I
 \oplus {1\over\sqrt2}\,I_{\ker S(\phi)^*}.         \tag{3}
\]

The summand \(A_f\) is cyclic and irreducible and

\[
 W(A_f)=\overline{\mathbb D}.                      \tag{4}
\]

Moreover, its dimension is \(pr\) for some
\(1\le r\le m\).  After a near-identity unitary alignment (and then
an inessential copy-unitary gauge), \(T'\) is near
\(C_p\otimes I_{m-r}\) and is again a numerical contraction.  Thus a
terminal scalar equality state removes at least one copy's worth of
the repeated block; the remaining problem has strictly smaller copy
multiplicity.  L328 subsequently sharpens the local conclusion to
\(r=1\): a larger irreducible Gau--Wu model cannot approach several
short Crabb chains.

Applied to L325, (1) is exactly

\[
 \|P^{-1/2}f(C)P^{1/2}x\|=2,
 \qquad C=P^{1/2}TP^{-1/2}.                        \tag{5}
\]

Therefore L325's partial-endpoint zero face is a reducing scalar
model summand.  No new finite-observability splitting lemma is
needed.

## 2. Classical equality theorem

Gau and Wu, *Inner functions of numerical contractions*, Linear
Algebra Appl. **430** (2009), 2182--2191,
DOI `10.1016/j.laa.2008.11.020`, prove the following.

- Their Theorem 8 says that a numerical contraction with no singular
  unitary part satisfying (1) has precisely the reducing summand
  (2)--(3), and that \(f\) is inner with \(f(0)=0\).
- Their Theorem 5 says that the displayed model block is cyclic,
  irreducible, has no unitary part, has numerical range the closed
  disk, and attains two for \(f\).

A sufficiently small neighbourhood of \(T_0\) contains no matrix
with a reducing unitary summand.  Otherwise a convergent sequence of
unitary reducing vectors would give a unitary reducing vector for
the nilpotent \(T_0\).  Thus Gau--Wu's “no singular unitary part”
hypothesis is automatic here.

Because \(T\) is finite-dimensional, (2) also forces
\(\dim H(\phi)<\infty\), so \(\phi\) is a finite Blaschke product.
No claim that \(f\) is a monomial is made for a general L193 disk
point.

## 3. Local copy bookkeeping

Let \(Q\) be the reducing projection onto \(A_f\).  If no uniform
local rank statement held, there would be

\[
 T_n\longrightarrow T_0,\qquad
 [T_n,Q_n]=[T_n^*,Q_n]=0,
\]

with \(\operatorname{rank}Q_n\) not divisible by \(p\).  Passing to
a subsequence gives a projection \(Q\) reducing \(T_0\) with the same
rank.

The single Crabb block is irreducible, so the \(C^*\)-algebra
generated by \(C_p\) is \(M_p\).  Consequently the reducing
projections of

\[
 C_p\otimes I_m
\]

are exactly \(I_p\otimes q\), up to the fixed tensor convention.
Every such projection has rank \(p\operatorname{rank}q\), a
contradiction.  Hence

\[
 \dim H(\phi)=\operatorname{rank}Q=pr.             \tag{6}
\]

The same compactness argument makes \(Q\) uniformly close to the
product reducing-projection set.  A copy-unitary then aligns it with
\(I_p\otimes q\).  Compression to \(Q^\perp\) proves that \(T'\) is
near \(C_p\otimes I_{m-r}\).  Since numerical radius does not increase
under a reducing compression, \(w(T')\le1\).

This already gives a finite multiplicity descent.  L328 adds the
one-copy conclusion needed to place every separated model directly
inside L192's single-block tube.  Neither statement claims that the
general model summand equals the nilpotent Crabb block.

## 4. Nilpotent specialization and correction of a tempting shortcut

If, in addition, the physical \(T\) is nilpotent of index at most
\(p\), then every zero of \(\phi\) is zero.  Hence

\[
 \phi(z)=e^{i\alpha}z^d,\qquad
 f(z)=e^{i\alpha}z^{d-1}.                          \tag{7}
\]

Equation (6) and the nilpotency index force \(d=p\), so \(r=1\), and
\(A_f\) is unitarily equivalent to \(C_p\).  In the polynomial basis
\((1,z,\ldots,z^{p-1})\), (3) has diagonal

\[
 (1/\sqrt2,1,\ldots,1,\sqrt2),
\]

and its similarity of the forward shift has weights

\[
 (\sqrt2,1,\ldots,1,\sqrt2).                      \tag{8}
\]

This monomial conclusion is valid on the nilpotent specialization
only.  A general L193 disk point need not be nilpotent, so using (7)
there would be an invalid strengthening.  The reducing model theorem
(2), rather than monomial rigidity, is the actual terminal argument.

## 5. Consequence for the finite scalar induction

At a nonzero L325 joint defect, its linear reserve is available and
L324's stacked response completion is quadratic.  At zero joint
defect with scalar norm equality, (2) removes a classical disk-model
summand and strictly decreases \(m\).  L328 shows that it removes
exactly one copy and lies in L192's single-block tube.  The base
\(m=1\) disk summand is already handled by (4), while L192 supplies
the campaign's channel-preserving local response.

Thus the terminal equality stratum is no longer an open
model-kernel debt.  What remains is the quantitative finite merger:
use L327's comparison of L324's response coordinates with L325's
directionwise defect, retain L318's elliptic margin, and assemble the
rank-stratified scalar estimate without spending a disk square twice.
