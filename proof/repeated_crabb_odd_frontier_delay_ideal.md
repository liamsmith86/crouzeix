# Every lower odd return face lies in the next delay ideal

## 1. Result (L277, 2026-07-25)

Let

\[
E=I-S^*S,\qquad F=I-SS^*,\qquad EF=0,
\]

and use L258's unweighted final-row renewal

\[
\mathfrak U=I-Z_{\rm ret}.
\]

For \(h\geq1\), impose only the lower delays

\[
ES^jF=0\qquad(1\leq j<h)                         \tag{1}
\]

and put

\[
\Omega_h=[c^{2h+1}]\mathfrak U.
\]

Then \(\Omega_h\) belongs to the two-sided ideal generated by the
next delay and its adjoint:

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_h\in
\langle ES^hF,\;F(S^*)^hE\rangle.}              \tag{2}
\]

Consequently, on every completely delayed grade \(k\),

\[
\boxed{
[c^{2h+1}]\mathfrak U_k=0
\quad(0\leq h<k).}                               \tag{3}
\]

The retained boundary metric is even in \(c\), so L258's weighted
closed defect

\[
\mathfrak D_k=I-R_{P,k}Z_{{\rm ret},k}
\]

also satisfies

\[
\boxed{
[c^d]\mathfrak D_k=0
\quad(d<2k,\ d\ {\rm odd}).}                    \tag{4}
\]

This closes the odd-face premise of L276 in arbitrary grade.  L278
subsequently proves every lower even face vanishes as well.  The
active cyclic-radial envelope is the sole remaining support statement
on the A223 route.

## 2. The universal odd frontier

Write \(s=S\), \(a=S^*\).  In the quotient (1), for every \(h\geq2\)
the frontier has the following twelve-word form:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Omega_h={}&
2a^{h-1}s^{h+2}a+2sa^{h+2}s^{h-1}\\
&+s^ha^{h+2}+s^{h+2}a^h
 +s^{h+1}a^{h+3}+s^{h+3}a^{h+1}\\
&-a^2s^{h+2}a^{h+2}
 -as^{h+2}a^{h+3}\\
&-s^{h+2}a^{h+2}s^2
 -s^{h+3}a^{h+2}s\\
&-2sa^{h+2}s^{h+2}a^3
 -2s^3a^{h+2}s^{h+2}a .
\end{aligned}}                                   \tag{5}
\]

This is an arbitrary-grade first-visit recursion, not a formula
interpolated from the finite exact audits.

Let \(P=I-F\), \(T=SP\), and let \(\iota\) substitute the independently
balanced tail variables \(T,T^*,P\) for \(s,a,I\).  After L258 has
summed all final-row loops, grouping by the first and last visit to
the removed row gives, for \(h\geq3\),

\[
\boxed{\Omega_h=\iota(\Omega_{h-1})+\Lambda_h,}   \tag{6}
\]

where the complete new-crossing layer is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda_h={}&
2a^{h-1}s^{h+2}a-2a^{h-2}s^{h+2}a^2\\
&+2sa^{h+2}s^{h-1}-2s^2a^{h+2}s^{h-2}\\
&-2sa^{h+2}s^{h+2}a^3
 -2s^3a^{h+2}s^{h+2}a\\
&+2s^2a^{h+2}s^{h+2}a^4
 +2s^4a^{h+2}s^{h+2}a^2 .
\end{aligned}                                    \tag{7}
\]

The no-new-crossing paths in (6) are the tail paths with their clean
entry/exit layer removed; their coefficient degree drops by two.
For a new crossing, mark its first entrance and last exit.  L242's
universal relative-one direct jet, L245's exact exterior Green
columns, and L258's already-resummed final-row inverse leave the
following exhaustive normal forms:

\[
\begin{array}{c|r}
\text{new-crossing normal word}&\text{complete coefficient}\\ \hline
a^{h-1}s^{h+2}a&2\\
a^{h-2}s^{h+2}a^2&-2\\
sa^{h+2}s^{h-1}&2\\
s^2a^{h+2}s^{h-2}&-2\\
sa^{h+2}s^{h+2}a^3&-2\\
s^3a^{h+2}s^{h+2}a&-2\\
s^2a^{h+2}s^{h+2}a^4&2\\
s^4a^{h+2}s^{h+2}a^2&2 .
\end{array}                                      \tag{8}
\]

Here “complete” is load-bearing: the direct, immediate-return, and
positive-loop pieces do not obey (8) separately.  To obtain (8),
insert L242's simplified relative-one jet

\[
\mathcal D_0=SF,\qquad
\mathcal D_1=FJ-S^3F,
\quad J=3a-a^2s-sa^2,
\]

into L245's two exterior columns and then perform L258's closed
renewal before reducing words.  Reading maximal \(s/a\) runs gives
exactly (8).  Any other placement either:

1. contains \(ES^jF\) or its adjoint for \(j<h\);
2. has no new crossing and belongs to the tail term in (6); or
3. uses another reflected selection and lies above degree \(2h+1\).

Thus the table is exhaustive at arbitrary \(h\).  Direct reduction
of the \(h=2\) table gives (5) at the base.  Substituting

\[
T^j=S^{j+1}S^*,\qquad
(T^*)^j=S(S^*)^{j+1}
\]

into (5) at \(h-1\), and adding (7), gives (5) at \(h\).  This proves
(5) by induction.

## 3. Exact delay-ideal factorization

Define the next unreduced delay generators

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_h&=Es^hF
=as^{h+2}a+s^h-s^{h+1}a-as^{h+1},\\
D_h^*&=Fa^hE
=sa^{h+2}s+a^h-a^{h+1}s-sa^{h+1}.
\end{aligned}                                    \tag{9}
\]

For every \(h\geq2\), expansion using only the partial-isometry
relations and the lower delays (1) gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Omega_h={}&
-2sa^{h+1}D_ha^2-2s^3a^{h+1}D_h\\
&-aD_ha^{h+1}-D_ha^{h+2}\\
&-2s^2D_h^*s^h-s^{h+1}D_h^*s-s^{h+2}D_h^*\\
&+2a^{h-2}D_h .
\end{aligned}}                                   \tag{10}
\]

This can also be checked directly by expanding the eight products:
after the lower-delay and \(sas=s,\ asa=a,\ EF=0\) reductions, the
surviving terms are precisely the twelve terms in (5).

The exceptional \(h=1\) face has eight words rather than twelve, but
it factors in the same ideal:

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_1=
-3sa^2D_1a^2-3s^3a^2D_1-D_1a^3
-3s^2D_1^*s-s^3D_1^*.}                          \tag{11}
\]

Equations (10)--(11) prove (2).  Adding the next complete delay
\(D_h=0\) proves (3), including the degree-three base.

## 4. Passage from the unweighted renewal to the closed defect

On the retained space,

\[
\mathfrak D
=I-R_PZ_{\rm ret}
=(I-R_P)+R_P\mathfrak U.                         \tag{12}
\]

Every coefficient of \(R_P\) has even \(c\)-degree.  Fix an odd
\(d<2k\).  Each odd coefficient of \(\mathfrak U\) entering the
convolution in (12) has the form \(2h+1\leq d\), hence \(h<k\), and
vanishes by (3).  The first term of (12) is even.  Therefore the
degree-\(d\) coefficient of \(\mathfrak D_k\) is zero, proving (4).
