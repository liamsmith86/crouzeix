## 1. The theorem

Fix `p=L+1` and one phase chart of L123's exact disk equality
family.  Use one coefficient `u_k` from each reflected pair
`1<=k<=L/2`, with the central coefficient real in the fixed phase
gauge.  Let

\[
 A(u,c)=X(u)+cX(u)^*,\qquad
 T(u,c)=\phi_c(A(u,c)),
\]

and let `Gamma_eq(u,c)` be L118's locally optimized rank-one Stein
envelope minus four on this exact family.

There is a fixed neighbourhood, independent of the relative sizes of
`u` and `c`, in which

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm eq}(u,c)
\le
-8c^{2L}
-32\sum_{k\le L/2}|u_k|^2c^{2k}
<0
\qquad(c\ne0).
}                                                     \tag{1}
\]

At `c=0`, L123 gives equality `Gamma_eq(u,0)=0`.  L149--L150 prove
(1), hence a full elliptic tube over the whole local disk-equality
stratum.  It is not the merger with directions normal to that
stratum.

## 3. The reflected-ideal gate identified by the audit

Put

\[
 w_k=c^ku_k,\qquad
 {\cal N}(u,c)=c^{2L}+\sum_k|w_k|^2.                 \tag{3}
\]

The audit isolated the lemma that the analytic expansion must obey
the following support rule.  If a Taylor monomial contains at least
two equality coefficients with grades `k_1,...,k_r`, ordered so that
`k_1<=k_2<=...`, then its `c`-valuation is at least

\[
 k_1+k_2.                                             \tag{4}
\]

Equivalently, after subtracting the pure axis, the expansion belongs
to the convergent Newton ideal generated by the weighted quadratic
monomials

\[
c^{k+\ell}u_k\overline{u_\ell}
\quad\text{and their real/complex polarizations}.     \tag{5}
\]

Introduce the reflection variables

\[
 r=c^L,\qquad w_k=c^ku_k.                             \tag{5a}
\]

L131 gives precisely these negative Hardy legs, while L123 resums
every unreflected insertion into the exact disk-equality factor.
After recentering the defect at L123's exact analytic rank-one metric,
the desired statement is

\[
\Gamma_{\rm eq}\in
(r,w,\overline w)^2
\quad\hbox{in the convergent reflected Rees algebra}. \tag{5b}
\]

The first draft listed two apparent proof obligations:

1. extend L143's coefficientwise Weierstrass preparation to arbitrary
   amplitude degree and show that projection onto the degree-`<L`
   factor preserves the reflected ideal after the L123 recentering;
2. insert that prepared factor into the full Stein critical equations
   and prove that solving by L118's unit defect Hessian preserves
   `(r,w,conjugate(w))^2`, not merely its degree-two associated face.

The heuristic was strong: a scalar endpoint contribution must leave
the exact equality factor and return, requiring two reflected legs,

## 4. Consequence of the reflected-ideal lemma

The pure-axis expansion from L117 is

\[
\Gamma_{\rm eq}(0,c)
=-16c^{2L}+O(c^{4L}).                                 \tag{6}
\]

Equations (2), (4), and L146 identify every compact face coefficient:

\[
\Gamma_{\rm eq}(u,c)
=-16c^{2L}
-64\sum_k|u_k|^2c^{2k}
+R(u,c),                                              \tag{7}
\]

where every monomial of `R` is strictly above the Newton face in
(7).  In particular:

1. an extra power of `c` is above the face;
2. an extra amplitude factor is above the corresponding weighted
   quadratic generator; and
3. distinct-grade quadratic terms on the face vanish by L146.

This is the all-order statement missing from the earlier
coefficientwise Hessian calculations.

## 5. Uniform domination

For completeness, the standard Newton estimate is elementary here.
Choose from each monomial in `R` two amplitude occurrences of least
grade.  Factor their weighted product `w_k w_\ell`; every remaining
amplitude factor is bounded by `||u||`, and every excess `c` factor is
bounded by `|c|`.  Then

\[
 |w_kw_\ell|
 \le {1\over2}(|w_k|^2+|w_\ell|^2).                  \tag{8}
\]

Convergence of the analytic series gives a common constant `C` such
that, on a sufficiently small polydisk of radius `delta`,

\[
 |R(u,c)|
 \le C\delta\left(
 c^{2L}+\sum_k|w_k|^2
 \right).                                             \tag{9}
\]

The pure-axis remainder in (6) satisfies the same estimate after
shrinking `delta`.  Choose it so that `C delta<=8`.  Equations
(7)--(9) then give (1).

The argument is uniform over all approaches to the origin: no
assumption such as `u=O(c^m)` or `c=O(||u||^m)` is made.
