# The stratified normal form of the disk equality cone (2026-07-23)

## 1. Result

Let `n=L-1` and let

\[
 {\cal C}z=J\overline z
\]

be reversal conjugation on `C^n`.  L122's disk-flat quartic is

\[
 {\cal Q}(z)=\|z\|^4-|z^TJz|^2,                      \tag{1}
\]

and L123 identifies its zero set

\[
 {\cal E}
 =\{z:z=\omega{\cal C}z\text{ for some }|\omega|=1\} \tag{2}
\]

with the exact disk equality family.

Every `z` has a best-phase splitting (unique in phase when
`z^TJz` is nonzero)

\[
 \boxed{z=u+v,\qquad
 {\cal T}u=u,\quad {\cal T}v=-v}                     \tag{3}
\]

for an antiunitary involution `T=eta C`, such that

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\|u\|^2&=\frac{\|z\|^2+|z^TJz|}{2},\\
\|v\|^2&=\frac{\|z\|^2-|z^TJz|}{2},\\
{\cal Q}(z)&=4\|u\|^2\|v\|^2,\\
\operatorname{dist}(z,{\cal E})&=\|v\|.
\end{aligned}}                                       \tag{4}
\]

Here `u` lies on the fixed-phase equality branch for `T`, while `v`
lies on the branch for `-T`.  The two summands are orthogonal in the
underlying real Euclidean space.

Consequently, if `d=dist(z,E)`, then

\[
 \boxed{
 {\cal Q}(z)=4d^2(\|z\|^2-d^2),\qquad
 2\|z\|^2d^2\leq{\cal Q}(z)\leq4\|z\|^2d^2.}          \tag{5}
\]

The lower bound uses `d^2<=||z||^2/2`, which is automatic for the
best-phase splitting.

This is the correct replacement for the false coercive quartic from
L121.  Away from the Crabb apex, (5) is quadratic in the normal
distance to a fixed-phase equality branch.  At the apex its coefficient
vanishes quadratically, explaining why an ordinary smooth
Morse--Bott chart cannot be uniform there.

## 2. Construction

Use the standard inner product, linear in the second argument, and put

\[
 a=\|z\|^2,\qquad \beta=z^TJz.
\]

If `beta` is nonzero, choose

\[
 \eta=\frac{\beta}{|\beta|};
\]

if `beta=0`, choose any unimodular `eta`.  Then

\[
 \langle z,\eta{\cal C}z\rangle=|\beta|.
\]

Since `C` is an antiunitary involution, so is

\[
 {\cal T}=\eta{\cal C}.
\]

Define

\[
 u=\frac{z+{\cal T}z}{2},\qquad
 v=\frac{z-{\cal T}z}{2}.                            \tag{6}
\]

Antilinearity causes no issue because the coefficients in (6) are
real.  Directly,

\[
 {\cal T}u=u,\qquad {\cal T}v=-v.
\]

Thus `u` belongs to the `eta` branch in (2), and `v` belongs to the
`-eta` branch.

## 3. Norms and the quartic

Expanding (6) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|u\|^2
 &=\frac14\left(2a+
    2\operatorname{Re}\langle z,{\cal T}z\rangle\right)
  =\frac{a+|\beta|}{2},\\
\|v\|^2
 &=\frac14\left(2a-
    2\operatorname{Re}\langle z,{\cal T}z\rangle\right)
  =\frac{a-|\beta|}{2}.                              \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]

Also `Re <u,v>=0`, so (3) is orthogonal over the real scalars.
Multiplying the two identities in (7) proves

\[
4\|u\|^2\|v\|^2=a^2-|\beta|^2={\cal Q}(z).           \tag{8}
\]

This also reproves the equality classification:
`Q(z)=0` if and only if one of `u,v` vanishes, equivalently
`z=omega C z` for a phase `omega`.

## 4. Exact distance to the union of branches

For a fixed unimodular `gamma`, the real orthogonal projection onto

\[
 {\cal E}_\gamma=\{x:x=\gamma{\cal C}x\}
\]

is

\[
 \Pi_\gamma z=\frac{z+\gamma{\cal C}z}{2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{dist}(z,{\cal E}_\gamma)^2
=\frac{a-\operatorname{Re}
  \{\gamma\overline\beta\}}{2}.                      \tag{9}
\]

Minimizing (9) over `gamma` selects
`gamma=beta/|beta|` and gives

\[
\operatorname{dist}(z,{\cal E})^2
=\frac{a-|\beta|}{2}=\|v\|^2.                        \tag{10}
\]

Equations (7)--(10) prove (4)--(5).

## 5. Consequence for the local merger

Write a disk-flat coefficient as `z=u+v` using (3), with
`||v||<=||u||`.  L123 controls `u` nonperturbatively: moving along its
fixed-phase branch remains at exact `t_*=4`.  L122 supplies the first
normal disk deficit

\[
 -32{\cal Q}(z)=-128\|u\|^2\|v\|^2.                 \tag{11}
\]

Thus the remaining variables should be organized as

\[
(\text{equality amplitude }u,
 \text{disk normal }v,
 \text{elliptic normal }c),
\]

not as one undifferentiated disk-flat vector.  A uniform proof must
combine:

1. quadratic normal control `-||u||^2||v||^2` when the equality
   amplitude is visible;
2. L117's apex control `-c^(2L)` when `u` is tiny; and
3. the elliptic deficit generated by a nonzero equality amplitude.
