# First-reflection trace locality for the delayed volume

## 1. Candidate theorem (A194 synthesis, 2026-07-25)

Let \(S\) be a finite pure partial isometry with equal orthogonal
defects and square matrix-inner transfer

\[
B(z)=\sum_{j\geq1}B_jz^j.
\]

Assume complete delay

\[
B_1=\cdots=B_{k-1}=0.
\]

Use L250/L264's port-isolated right-half-line gauge and L258's closed
return \(\mathfrak D_{\rm ret}\).  The proposed conclusion is

\[
[c^d]\operatorname {tr}\mathfrak D_{\rm ret}=0
\quad(d<2k),                                      \tag{1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
[c^{2k}]\operatorname {tr}\mathfrak D_{\rm ret}
=4\|B_k\|_F^2.}                                  \tag{2}
\]

This would close A194.  L247 would then add the already proved metric
contribution \(-2\|B_k\|_F^2\), giving the total delayed Schur trace
\(2\|B_k\|_F^2\).

The proposed proof below reduces (1)--(2) to a precise trace-ideal
statement.  Its ingredients are banked exact formulas, but the
assembly in Sections 3--4 is **not** banked: it is exactly what must be
proved.  Section 6 lists the two points that must survive an
independent attack before this note may be promoted to a lemma.

## 2. Work after the complete background cancellation

Put \(r=k-1\).  L264 permits the complete right-half-line metric
\(D_R\) to be restored while the active left orbit and the final
corner/cross row remain deleted.  L258 eliminates the final row and
resums every visit to it:

\[
Z_{\rm ret}=PZP+PZF(I-FZF)^{-1}FZP,\qquad
\mathfrak D_{\rm ret}=I-R_PZ_{\rm ret}.           \tag{3}
\]

For the half-line background, L244 gives
\(R_PZ_{\rm ret}=I\), so every copy-free term in (3) cancels before
coefficient extraction.

L263 then permits the first genuinely new Schur face to be read in
constant Wold coordinates.  Motion of the defect graph, fixed port,
metric, and Wold basis is one order too late.  Thus the active trace
may be expanded using

\[
U_0=L^*,\qquad {\cal W}_0=I                       \tag{4}
\]

after the complete physical numerator has been assembled.

This order is essential: L260 forbids separating the two ellipse
orientations, and L249 forbids varying the doubled terminal edge away
from its physical value.

## 3. Proposed leakage-ideal placement (open)

Let

\[
{\cal L}_B={\cal T}_B{\cal T}_B^*
\]

on \(H^2(\mathbb C^m)\), and let \({\cal A}\) be the copy-scalar
algebra generated by \(L,L^*\) and finite boundary projections.

The exact L243 resolvent has the sole non-scalar middle factor

\[
K_{z^rB}(\rho,x)^{-1},\qquad
K_{z^rB}
=\sum_{a=0}^{r-1}t^aI+t^rK_B.                    \tag{5}
\]

L243's filtration says that only the zero and first nonconstant
inverse-kernel sectors can reach degree \(2r+2=2k\).  The zero sector
is copy-free and cancels by Section 2.  In the first sector all
transfer dependence occurs in the ordered product
\(B(\rho)B^\sharp(x)\).

L254 and L259 identify its coefficient matrix with the complement of
the model projection:

\[
P_{{\cal K}_B}=I-{\cal L}_B,\qquad
P_i{\cal L}_BP_j
=\sum_{\ell<\min(i,j)}B_{i-\ell}B_{j-\ell}^*.    \tag{6}
\]

The scalar \(I\) part again belongs to the cancelled half-line
background.  The required conclusion is that every first nonconstant
tail term lies in the two-sided ideal

\[
{\cal A}{\cal L}_B{\cal A}.                       \tag{7}
\]

The banked ingredients suggesting that there should be no unpaired
copy matrix outside this ideal are:

1. L245's exterior chain columns are scalar continuant features.
2. L251 proves that every analytic tail-to-chain crossing is paired
   with a chain-to-tail crossing through the same ordered operator.
3. L258 makes those crossings closed before a trace is taken.
4. L262 turns the normalized intervening half-line metric into the
   ordinary Hardy metric.

What remains to be proved from those ingredients is that, after
cyclicity, every active scalar term has the form

\[
\operatorname {tr}
\{ {\cal L}_B\Phi(L,L^*){\cal L}_B\},             \tag{8}
\]

with \(\Phi\) copy-scalar.  Indeed, a term with one displayed
\({\cal L}_B\) may be replaced by \({\cal L}_B^2\) and cyclically
rotated; a second nonconstant inverse-kernel sector is excluded by
L243.

## 4. Proposed Hardy-row filtration modulo deep divergences (open)

It is not enough to count the length of raw words in \(L,L^*\).
Mixed unilateral resolvents can have boundary projections of
arbitrarily large depth.  The reason they do not appear outside
\({\cal L}_B\) here is the exact characteristic-kernel collapse, not
a naive word-length bound.

L239 gives

\[
W^*(I-\rho T^*)^{-1}(I-xT)^{-1}W
=\frac{I-tB(\rho)B^\sharp(x)}{1-t}.               \tag{9}
\]

For a single resolvent, the unbounded mixed half-line expression is
already the model kernel.  Its scalar part is removed by L244, and
its non-scalar part is absorbed into \({\cal L}_B\) through (6).  The
open issue is whether this absorption remains valid after multiplying
the two contour-resolvent factors in the physical
\(A R^{-1}A^*\) expression.

Conditional on that product-level absorption, what remains outside
\({\cal L}_B\) is finite.  L245's chain features
have distances \(d\leq r\), and L243's sole nonconstant kernel sector
can add only the first tail step.  The original proposed conclusion
was that, after normal ordering, every scalar shift word in
\(\Phi\) had boundary depth at most

\[
r+1=k.                                            \tag{10}
\]

L269 disproves that literal operator statement already at grade two.
The exact closed-return difference contains a nonzero depth-\(k+1\)
term \(G_k-SG_kS^*\).  Its delayed trace is zero, but neither word
vanishes in the operator quotient.  The corrected requirement is to
split every such deep term into a trace-zero Stein divergence (or
commutator) and apply L266 only to the remaining depth-at-most-\(k\)
part.  The point of using (9) first is still that a raw, uncollapsed
half-line resolvent has no controlled remainder at all.

The second required conclusion is a row filtration.  In a coefficient of
\({\cal L}_B\), an analytic transfer letter \(B_i\) comes from
\(\rho^iB_i\) and carries \(c\)-degree \(i\); its
\(B_j^*x^j\) partner may be a future column and carries no visible
\(c\)-degree.  If the two-sided Schur/closed-return form (8) holds, it
supplies two analytic leakage entries.  Hence a term anchored at Hardy rows
\(i,\ell\) has degree at least

\[
i+\ell.                                           \tag{11}
\]

Complete delay would then make \(i,\ell\geq k\), proving (1).  At degree
\(2k\), equality in (10) forces

\[
i=\ell=k.                                         \tag{12}
\]

All future adjoint columns remain in the complete \(k\)-th leakage
row; they are not discarded term by term.

Once the open placement claim and the corrected
shallow-plus-trace-null decomposition hold, L266 applies word by word
to the shallow part.  If \(\sigma_0(\Phi)\) is the constant
coefficient of its bilateral symbol, then

\[
\operatorname {tr}
\{P_k{\cal L}_B\Phi(L,L^*){\cal L}_BP_k\}
=\sigma_0(\Phi)\|B_k\|_F^2.                      \tag{13}
\]

Summing the finitely many active terms would give

\[
[c^{2k}]\operatorname {tr}\mathfrak D_{\rm ret}
=\alpha_k\|B_k\|_F^2,                            \tag{14}
\]

where \(\alpha_k\) depends only on the scalar physical
theta/continuant network, not on the tail or the copy dimension.

## 5. Conditional all-grade normalization by the exact monomial axis

Take the length-\(k\) monomial channel \(B(z)=z^kI_m\).  L241 proves
that its complete right-defect Schur face has trace \(2m\), with every
earlier face zero.  L246 transports that first face to the dual
orientation.  L247 proves

\[
\operatorname {tr}(\text{full}-\text{edge-deleted})=-2m.
\]

Therefore the edge-deleted/volume face is exactly \(4m\).  In (11),
\(\|B_k\|_F^2=m\), so

\[
\alpha_k=4
\]

for every \(k\).  Substitution in (14) then proves (2), conditional on
Sections 3--4.

This normalization uses the complete physical network at the same
grade.  It does not detach and square the terminal multiplier ruled
out by L249.

## 6. Stop conditions before promotion

Two claims above are load-bearing and must be checked directly
against the formulas, not accepted from the desired conclusion.

1. **Ideal placement.**  Expanding L258's renewal with L251's two
   port orientations must leave no one-sided
   \(B^\sharp(x)\), no copy-dependent renewal denominator, and no
   active term outside the trace ideal (7).  L243 allows one
   nonconstant inverse-kernel sector, but this must be counted across
   the complete \(A\,R^{-1}A^*\) product, not separately per
   resolvent.
2. **Depth/row filtration modulo divergences.**  The use of L239 in Section 4 must be
   checked after the two contour integrations implicit in
   \(A\,R^{-1}A^*\), not only inside one resolvent.  Every mixed
   half-line product must be absorbed into the model/leakage
   projection before the finite \(r+1\) depth count is applied.
   L269 proves that depth \(k+1\) words do survive, so the required
   theorem is that they assemble into delayed trace-zero Stein
   divergences before L266 is applied to the shallow remainder.

Existing exact word audits through grade seven and floating
full-pipeline audits through grade eight reproduce (1)--(2), including
generic noncommuting tails.  They are strong falsification tests but
do not replace these two all-grade checks.
