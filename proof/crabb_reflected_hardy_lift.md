# The reflected Hardy lift and uniform Crabb equality tube

## 1. Result (L149--L150, 2026-07-23)

Fix `L>=2` and one phase chart of L123's disk-equality family.  Let
`u_k`, `1<=k<=L/2`, be its independent low equality coefficients and
put

\[
r=c^L,\qquad w_k=c^ku_k,\qquad
{\cal N}=|r|^2+\sum_k|w_k|^2.                         \tag{1}
\]

For the exact equality/ellipse family

\[
A(u,c)=X(u)+cX(u)^*,\qquad
T(u,c)=\phi_c(A(u,c)),
\]

there is an explicit analytic rank-one Stein certificate
`U_mod(u,c)` such that

\[
t_*(T(u,c))-4\le U_{\rm mod}(u,c)-4
\]

and, in a fixed neighbourhood independent of relative scales,

\[
\boxed{
U_{\rm mod}(u,c)-4
\le-8|r|^2-32\sum_k|w_k|^2<0
\quad(c\ne0).
}                                                     \tag{2}
\]

Consequently L118's optimized envelope obeys the same upper bound.
This proves candidate L147: the complete elliptic tube over every
nearby exact disk-equality branch is strict.

The proof has two new parts.

1. **L149 (reflected Hardy lift).**  Every zero- or one-reflection
   term in the sharp prepared Blaschke norm vanishes.  Equivalently,
   its deficit belongs to the square of the convergent reflected
   ideal generated by `(r,w,conjugate(w))`.
2. **L150 (model-complement upper lift).**  L145's canonical
   orbit-complement defect extends analytically at arbitrary
   amplitude.  Its condition gap above the Blaschke lower bound is
   nonnegative and zero on the equality divisor, so it also belongs
   to the reflected ideal square.  Its compact quadratic face equals
   L146's negative face.

This closes the equality stratum only.  L124's best-phase normal and
L118's strong variables still have to be merged.

## 2. Exact reflected coordinates

Let

\[
g_u(\xi)=\xi^L+2\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}u_j\xi^j,\qquad
u_j=\overline {u_{L-j}}.
\]

L131's Faber identity is exact on the Joukowski boundary:

\[
\boxed{
({\cal F}_cg_u)(\zeta+c/\zeta)
=g_u(\zeta)+r\zeta^{-L}
+2\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}u_jc^j\zeta^{-j}.
}                                                     \tag{3}
\]

For a low representative `k`, the term with `j=L-k` is
`c^(L-2k)conjugate(w_k)zeta^{-(L-k)}`.  Thus every negative Hardy
coefficient in (3) is analytic in

\[
(u,c,r,w,\overline w),                               \tag{4}
\]

and belongs to the ideal

\[
{\mathfrak r}=(r,w,\overline w).                     \tag{5}
\]

The positive Hardy factor is exactly the L123 equality polynomial
`g_u`; it contains every unreflected amplitude insertion.

This gives a convergent, not merely formal, reflected lift.  Indeed:

1. monic Weierstrass division of
   `G(Psi_c)=N O` is triangular in scalar degree, so its coefficient
   map is analytic in (4);
2. the reversed denominator has unit constant term and its matrix
   evaluation is invertible near the equality divisor;
3. L125's direct-map recurrence, matrix polynomial evaluation, and
   the finite Stein/orbit equations use only sums, products, and
   inverses of units.

Consequently every matrix and scalar in the prepared Blaschke and
model-complement constructions has a convergent expansion in the
marked algebra

\[
{\cal A}
=\mathbb R\{u,\overline u,c,r,w,\overline w\},        \tag{6}
\]

whose pullback under (1) is the physical `(u,c)` family.
Projection to polynomial degree `<L` preserves the marking because
it is coefficientwise linear; multiplication adds reflection count.
This is the all-amplitude extension that was missing from the first
L147 draft.

Setting `mathfrak r=0` discards every negative Hardy leg in (3).
The remaining prepared realization is exactly L123's disk-equality
factor, up to the invertible outer factor `O`.  Hence every
zero-reflection certificate value is four for every small `u` and
benign parameter `c`.

## 3. The one-reflection Hardy transfer

It remains to show that one reflected leg cannot change the sharp
Blaschke norm to first order.  This is a model-space residue identity.

At a zero-reflection equality anchor write

\[
B=g/g^\sharp,\qquad B(A)=4e_0r_u^*,
\]

where `r_u=J conjugate(H e_0)`.  Its simple top right and left
singular lines are the endpoint defect lines from L123.

Let `delta` be any first variation in one of the independent negative
Hardy coefficients in (3), including a central fold.  Differentiate
both the prepared numerator and its coefficient realization.  The
top singular norming functional satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\frac14D_\delta\|B(T)\|^2
=2\operatorname{Re}
 \frac1{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=1}
 \frac{\dot\Theta(\zeta)}{\Theta(\zeta)}
 \frac{d\zeta}{\zeta},
\qquad \Theta(\zeta)=\zeta B(\zeta).
}                                                     \tag{7}
\]

Here `dot(Theta)` is the total first variation: numerator
preparation, reversed denominator, and the operator/Faber
realization are all included.

For completeness, the needed companion resolvent is explicit:

\[
\boxed{
q^*(zI-A)^{-1}e_L
=\frac{g^\sharp(z)}{z\,g(z)}
=\frac1{zB(z)}.
}                                                     \tag{7a}
\]

To verify (7a), multiply by `zI-A` and use the companion columns
(L123 equation (8)); the interior coefficients telescope, the last
column gives `g`, and the first endpoint row is exactly
`g^sharp`.  Equivalently it is the `(q,e_L)` entry of the adjugate
formula with `det(zI-A)=zg(z)`.

Cauchy's formula now sends a scalar numerator variation to

\[
q^*\dot B(A)e_L
=\frac1{2\pi i}\int
 \frac{\dot B(z)}{zB(z)}\,dz.
\]

For the simultaneous operator variation, differentiate the adjugate
identity (7a).  The two resolvent terms from `dot A` are precisely the
linearization of the prepared outer-coordinate conjugacy; after the
invertible outer factor cancels, the same logarithmic integral
remains with the **total** `dot Theta`.  This proves (7).  In marked
coefficient language, the statement is that the zero- plus
one-reflection sector is the tangent space to the companion model
with characteristic inner function `Theta`; failure of that model
identity requires two negative Hardy legs.  This is the basis-free
version of L148's explicit companion recurrence.

The prepared `Theta_delta` is a differentiable family of finite inner
functions of fixed degree.  On the circle,

\[
\operatorname{Re}
\frac{\dot\Theta(\zeta)}{\Theta(\zeta)}=0.            \tag{8}
\]

Therefore the real part of the integral in (7) is zero:

\[
\boxed{D_\delta\|B(T)\|^2=0.}                        \tag{9}
\]

This proof is insensitive to the Fourier grade, complex phase, or a
central collision.  If `g` has a multiple zero at the origin, prove
(7)--(9) after a generic phase-palindromic perturbation and pass to
the limit; all prepared coefficients and the simple endpoint
singular lines are analytic.

Equations (3), (6), and (9) show

\[
\boxed{
\|B(T)\|^2-4\in{\mathfrak r}^2.
}                                                     \tag{10}
\]

This is L149.  The exact finite-amplitude checker independently
confirms (10) through reflected grade three and dimension nine.

## 4. Canonical primal upper certificate

Use L145's construction at arbitrary nearby reflected data.  Let `x`
be the analytic simple top right singular vector of `B(T)` and
write `B=N/D`.  Choose the uniquely normalized nonzero vector

\[
q\perp D(T)^{-1}
\operatorname{span}\{x,Tx,\ldots,T^{L-1}x\}.         \tag{11}
\]

At the equality divisor the orbit has rank `L`; L123's observability
determinant is nonzero.  Hence (11) defines an analytic line
throughout a fixed neighbourhood.

Let

\[
P=P_T(q)=\sum_{n\ge0}(T^*)^nqq^*T^n,\qquad
U_{\rm mod}=\kappa(P).
\]

The model-kernel identity makes the Stein defect of `B(T)` annihilate
`x`, giving L145's exact complementary relation

\[
x^*Px=\|B(T)\|^2y^*Py.                               \tag{12}
\]

It follows that

\[
\Delta:=U_{\rm mod}-\|B(T)\|^2\ge0.                  \tag{13}
\]

On `mathfrak r=0`, (11) is L123's exact equality defect and
`U_mod=||B(T)||^2=4`.  The endpoint eigenvalues of `P` are simple.
Thus `Delta` is a real-analytic nonnegative function of the real and
imaginary reflected variables, vanishing for all small unreflected
`u,c`.  Its gradient on that divisor is zero, so

\[
\boxed{\Delta\in{\mathfrak r}^2.}                    \tag{14}
\]

This is the key simplification: the optimized Stein critical
recurrence does not have to be lifted to arbitrary amplitude.
The explicit feasible model-complement metric is enough.

## 5. Compact reflected face

The reflected quadratic face of `||B(T)||^2-4` is L142--L144:

\[
-16|r|^2-64\sum_k|w_k|^2.                            \tag{15}
\]

The model gap (13) has zero compact quadratic face.  For each fixed
small `c`, its amplitude Hessian is positive semidefinite because
`Delta>=0` and it vanishes at the axis.  L145 proves that every
one-grade diagonal coefficient at reflected weight `2k` is zero.
After grade congruence by `diag(c^(-k))`, the associated matrix is
positive semidefinite with zero diagonal, hence is zero.  The pure
axis diagonal is zero by L117, and the same `2x2` principal-minor
argument removes its cross terms.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
U_{\rm mod}-4
=-16|r|^2-64\sum_k|w_k|^2+R,
}                                                     \tag{16}
\]

where every monomial of `R` in the convergent algebra (6) is in
`mathfrak r^2` and lies strictly above the compact face: it has an
extra factor from `(u,c,mathfrak r)`.

## 6. Uniform domination

Put `eta=(r,w,conjugate(w))`.  Since (16) is convergent and its
quadratic coefficient matrix tends to the negative definite matrix
in (15),

\[
|R|\le C\delta\left(|r|^2+\sum_k|w_k|^2\right)
\]

on a sufficiently small polydisk of radius `delta`, uniformly over
all relative scales.  Choose `C delta<=8`.  Equation (16) gives (2).

There is no assumption relating `u` and `c`; in particular the proof
covers `c` exponentially smaller than every equality amplitude.
