# Two-layer normal form for every central inner fiber (2026-07-23)

## 1. Statement

Use L126's notation, put `r=c^k`, and remove the exact outer space

\[
 V=\operatorname{span}\{e_0,e_k,e_{2k}\}.
\]

Order the remaining coordinates in two layers

\[
 {\cal W}
 =\operatorname{span}\{e_1,\ldots,e_{k-1}\}
 \oplus
 \operatorname{span}\{e_{k+1},\ldots,e_{2k-1}\}.
\]

Let `J_(k-1)` reverse these `k-1` coordinates, set

\[
 D=\operatorname{diag}(c^{k-1},c^{k-2},\ldots,c),
\qquad H=J_{k-1}D.                                   \tag{1}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{H^2=rI}.                                     \tag{2}
\]

The exact inner restriction of L126's descended pencil is

\[
\boxed{
 P_k(S_k(a,c))|_{\cal W}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}H&I\\rI&H\end{pmatrix}
 +2a\begin{pmatrix}-rI&H\\H&-I\end{pmatrix}.}         \tag{3}
\]

The central Toeplitz coordinate Gramian restricts to

\[
\boxed{
 K_k(a)|_{\cal W}
 =\begin{pmatrix}I&2aI\\2aI&I\end{pmatrix}.}          \tag{4}
\]

Thus every one of the `2k-2` apparently unrelated inner coordinates
belongs to one two-layer algebra generated by the single involutive
variable `H/sqrt(r)`.

## 2. Proof

Write the layer coordinates as `L_j=e_j` and `R_j=e_(k+j)` for
`1<=j<k`.  L126's complete column formula gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_k(S)L_j&=c^{k-j}L_{k-j}+rR_j,\\
 P_k(S)R_j&=L_j+c^{k-j}R_{k-j}.                      \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]

Its complete derivative formula gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_kL_j&=-2rL_j+2c^{k-j}R_{k-j},\\
 D_kR_j&=2c^{k-j}L_{k-j}-2R_j.                       \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]

Equations (5)--(6) are exactly (3).  Moreover

\[
 (J D)^2
 =\operatorname{diag}
   (c^{k-j}c^j)_{j=1}^{k-1}
 =c^kI,
\]

which proves (2).

In `K=H_0+R^*H_0R`, the central lag contributes once at each outer
residue edge and twice at every inner residue edge.  Hence the two
inner copies of a fixed residue have Gram matrix
`[[1,2a],[2a,1]]`, with no coupling between distinct residues.  This
proves (4).

## 3. Symmetric involution gauge

Let

\[
 L_c=\operatorname{diag}(c^{1/2},c^{2/2},\ldots,
                          c^{(k-1)/2}).
\]

Then

\[
 L_c^{-1}HL_c=\sqrt r\,J_{k-1}.                      \tag{7}
\]

After this similarity, the operator part of (3) splits algebraically
over the `+/-1` eigenspaces of reversal into copies of

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \epsilon\sqrt r&1\\r&\epsilon\sqrt r
 \end{pmatrix}
 +2a\begin{pmatrix}
 -r&\epsilon\sqrt r\\
 \epsilon\sqrt r&-1
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \epsilon\in\{+1,-1\}.                        \tag{8}
\]

This is an algebraic similarity, not an orthogonal decomposition for
the original physical Gramian: `L_c^*L_c` does not commute with
reversal.  One must not infer the desired condition-number bound from
(8) alone.

## 4. Consequence for the metric-lift gate

L126/L127 no longer leave an arbitrary `(2k-2)`-dimensional inactive
fiber.  The missing upper certificate is a dimension-free
two-layer problem with one operator parameter satisfying `H^2=rI`.
The desired full metric should:

1. use the exact size-three metric on `V`;
2. solve the Stein inequality on (3)--(4);
3. have zero metric cross block between `V` and `W`; and
4. trap the generalized spectrum on `W` between the outer endpoints.

The numerical optimizer has precisely these four properties in the
local grid.  Equation (2) suggests proving them by a `2×2`
operator-valued Schur complement rather than by an induction over
