# Two-copy repeated-`C3` terminal tube (2026-07-22)

## 1. Setting

This note closes the uniformity gate left by L106--L109 on the sharp
two-copy terminal chart.  Let

\[
 M=I_2\otimes C_3,\qquad A=N+E,
\]

where `N` is the block-diagonal normal-stratum retraction from L88 and `E`
is the nonnormal two-copy Schur edge, including higher chart terms with the
same transverse factor.  Write

\[
 r=\|N-M\|,\qquad \delta=\|E\|,\qquad s=r+\delta.       \tag{1}
\]

At the sharp chart, after the unitary/circle normalizations of L100, the
leading copy matrix is

\[
 D=\begin{bmatrix}d&a\\0&-d\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 r=\epsilon|d|,\quad\delta=\epsilon a,
\]

and the normal second-order coordinate is
`w=3d^2/(8sqrt(2))`.  Deviations from this center are already strict by
L95--L100; Sections 2--6 treat the center uniformly as `a/|d|` tends to
zero.

## 2. The actual and normal domains are quadratically close

For a boundary direction `q`, let `K_0(q)` be the Hermitian support matrix
of `M`, let `P(q)` be its repeated top spectral projection, and let `V_E(q)`
be the support perturbation generated by `E`.  L88's flat-copy identity
starts with the exact cancellation

\[
 P(q)V_E(q)P(q)=0.                                    \tag{2}
\]

The top cluster of `K_0(q)` has a uniform gap from the remaining
eigenvalues.  The corresponding cluster projection for the normal matrix
`N` differs from `P(q)` by `O(r)`, uniformly in `q`.  Consequently

\[
 \|P_N(q)V_E(q)P_N(q)\|\le C r\delta.                 \tag{3}
\]

The off-cluster Feshbach term is bounded by `C delta^2`.  Equations
(2)--(3), or equivalently the uniform cluster Schur complement, therefore
give

\[
 h_A(q)\le h_N(q)+C\delta(r+\delta).                  \tag{4}
\]

Every diagonal block of `N` is a compression of `A`; since numerical ranges
are convex,

\[
 W(N)\subset W(A),\qquad h_N(q)\le h_A(q).             \tag{5}
\]

Thus, with `Omega_N=int W(N)` and `Omega_A=int W(A)`,

\[
 0\le h_A-h_N\le C\delta s.                           \tag{6}
\]

This estimate is crossing-stable: it uses the whole isolated top cluster,
not a selected largest-eigenvalue branch.

## 3. Whole-normal composition and a small Stein slack

Normalize the Riemann maps at zero with positive derivative:

\[
 f_N:\Omega_N\longrightarrow\mathbb D,\qquad
 f_A:\Omega_A\longrightarrow\mathbb D.
\]

Because all nearby domains contain a fixed disk and (6) is a support
estimate,

\[
 \Omega_N\subset\Omega_A
 \subset(1+C\delta s)\Omega_N.                        \tag{7}
\]

Let `psi_N=f_N^{-1}` and

\[
 h=f_A\circ\psi_N:\mathbb D\longrightarrow\mathbb D. \tag{8}
\]

It fixes zero.  Monotonicity and scaling of conformal radius in (7) give

\[
 1-C\delta s\le h'(0)\le1.                            \tag{9}
\]

For completeness, write `h(z)=zg(z)`.  Schwarz's lemma gives `|g|\le1`,
and the Schwarz--Pick disk with center `g(0)=h'(0)` gives, on every fixed
compact subdisk,

\[
 \sup|h(z)-z|\le C\delta s.                           \tag{10}
\]

This is a linear, not square-root, stability estimate.

Put

\[
 S=f_N(N),\qquad T_N=f_A(N)=h(S).                     \tag{11}
\]

L88 gives a complete-`2` metric for `S`; L107--L108 put it on the
zero-Stein-slack chart.  Denote it by

\[
 P_0=P(S,B_0,0),\qquad I\preceq P_0\preceq4I.         \tag{12}
\]

Regard `S` as a contraction in the `P_0` inner product.  Von Neumann's
inequality applied to the disk self-map `h` shows that the **same** `P_0`
contracts `T_N=h(S)`.  Its lower and upper bounds are unchanged.  Its Stein
Schur complement for `T_N` is a block-diagonal matrix `H_delta>=0`, and
local uniqueness in L107 gives

\[
 P_0=P(T_N,B_0,H_\delta),\qquad
 {\cal E}(T_N,B_0,H_\delta)\preceq0.                  \tag{13}
\]

The spectra in (11) remain in a fixed compact subdisk.  Applying (10) by
the holomorphic functional calculus, then using analyticity of a Schur
complement, yields the weighted estimate

\[
 \boxed{\|H_\delta\|\le C\delta s.}                   \tag{14}
\]

Unlike the per-block slack in L106, this whole-normal slack vanishes when
`E=0`; it measures only enlargement from `W(N)` to `W(A)`.

## 4. A weighted analytic-remainder lemma

Let `F` be any finite-dimensional real-analytic matrix function near the
origin.  Split its variables into normal variables `u`, transverse
variables `v`, and a Stein-slack variable `H`, with

\[
 \|u\|\le Cs,\qquad \|v\|\le C\delta,\qquad
 \|H\|\le C\delta s.                                  \tag{15}
\]

In

\[
 F(u+v,0)-F(u,H),                                     \tag{16}
\]

every Taylor monomial contains either `v` or `H`.  Give `u,v` weight one
and `H` weight two.  After retaining all monomials of weighted degree at
most three, absolute convergence of the Taylor series gives

\[
 \boxed{\|R\|\le C\delta s^3.}                        \tag{17}
\]

Indeed, the remaining terms containing `v` are bounded by
`C delta s^3`; those linear in `H` are bounded by
`C(delta s)s^2`; and terms containing `H^2` are bounded by
`C delta^2s^2<=C delta s^3`.  The same proof is uniform when bounded
holomorphic functions enter only through fixed-contour functional calculus,
because the contour estimates make the family of analytic functions
locally bounded.

Apply this lemma to L105's endpoint.  L106 gives

\[
 T_A=f_A(A)=T_N+X,\qquad \|X\|\le C\delta.             \tag{18}
\]

Choose the free block

\[
 B_A=B_0+Y,\qquad \|Y\|\le C\delta,                   \tag{19}
\]

whose weighted jet is the canonical L98/L109 metric jet.  Higher
coefficients may be set to zero; the exact analytic chart supplies the
remaining metric.  Equations (14), (18), and (19) put

\[
 {\cal G}:={\cal E}(T_A,B_A,0)
            -{\cal E}(T_N,B_0,H_\delta)               \tag{20}
\]

under (17).

## 5. Identification of the retained jet

The support-cluster Feshbach expansion used in L98--L100 is uniform in `q`.
After subtracting the normal chart, its scalar second coefficient is

\[
 \Delta m={5a^2\over128},                             \tag{21}
\]

and its first non-scalar coefficient has mean top eigenvalue `m_3` satisfying

\[
 m_3\ge {a(a^2+2d^2)\over64}.                         \tag{22}
\]

The cluster remainder after these terms is
`O(delta s^3)` in support norm.  L101's radial/logarithmic argument applies
after the scalar second-order recentering: the residual perturbation scale
is `delta s^2`, its normalized profiles form a compact continuous family,
and the remainder divided by that scale is `O(s)`.  It therefore supplies
the same coefficients in the Riemann map with an
`o(delta s^2)` remainder, without choosing a smooth support branch.

Here is the uniform convex-duality point in that invocation.  Put
`eta=delta s^2`.  After removing the already shared lower-order support
terms, write the actual/normal support difference as

\[
 \eta q_s(t)+R_s(t),\qquad
 \|R_s\|_\infty\le C\delta s^3=o(\eta),                \tag{22a}
\]

where the profiles `q_s` are uniformly Lipschitz.  In the radial formula
`rho(t)=min_u h(u)/cos(u-t)`, every minimizer for either near-disk support
lies within `O(sqrt(s))` of `t`.  Use the normal minimizer for the upper
comparison and the actual minimizer for the lower comparison.  Uniform
equicontinuity of `q_s` then turns (22a) into the same
`eta q_s(t)+o(eta)` radial difference.  After factoring the common
lower-order normalized map, Section 3 of L101 applies verbatim to the
logarithm of the quotient map.  This proves the stated locally uniform
Riemann-map remainder and is insensitive to corners or changes of active
support eigenvector.

L109 computes (20) with an arbitrary normal scalar coefficient, arbitrary
cubic coefficient, arbitrary normal third mean, and arbitrary first
conformal coefficient.  All common coefficients cancel.  With a retained
slack fraction `theta`, its two relevant coefficients are

\[
 {\cal G}_2={5a^2\over8}I-16\Delta m\,I=0,\qquad
 {\cal G}_3=-16m_3I.                                  \tag{23}
\]

The mixed cubic entries in the true inherited slack are essential for
(23); the diagonal-only shortcut is false at the metric level.

Restoring physical scales in (22)--(23) and using (17) gives

\[
 \lambda_{\max}{\cal G}
 \le-{\delta(\delta^2+2r^2)\over4}
     +o\{\delta(r^2+\delta^2)\}
     +C\delta s^3.                                    \tag{24}
\]

Since

\[
 \delta^2+2r^2\ge {2\over3}(r+\delta)^2,              \tag{25}
\]

the right side of (24) is negative for all sufficiently small `s`, uniformly
in the ratio `delta/r`.

Finally, the second endpoint in (20) is nonpositive by (13).  Hence

\[
 \boxed{{\cal E}(T_A,B_A,0)\preceq0}                  \tag{26}
\]

throughout a punctured neighbourhood of the sharp two-copy terminal center.
At `delta=0`, L88 supplies the exact normal-stratum result.

## 6. Completion of the two-copy terminal chart

L95--L100 are strict on every normalized weighted chart away from the sharp
center.  On a compact set separated from that center, their leading
negative coefficient has a uniform margin and the same analytic estimate
absorbs the remainder.  Equation (26) covers the only collapsing
normal-face patch.  A finite compactness cover therefore proves:

\[
\boxed{\text{Every sufficiently small two-copy terminal block in L93's
flat core has the complete-`2` similarity bound.}}     \tag{27}
\]

This is a two-copy terminal-tube theorem, not yet the full repeated-block
neighbourhood theorem.  The remaining work is to make the metric-flag
reduction of L93 uniform in arbitrary copy multiplicity and then merge it
with L86's losing-space gaps.
