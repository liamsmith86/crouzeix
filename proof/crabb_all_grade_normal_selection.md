## 2. Exact first-column theorem

Let `C_p` be the Crabb matrix.  Compare:

1. the first normalized Stein-metric variation generated by `N_m`;
2. the variation generated by the defect coordinate `e_m`.

Call the resulting Hermitian matrices `P_N` and `P_q`.  Then

\[
\boxed{P_N=\gamma_{L,k}P_q+R_{L,k}},                 \tag{3}
\]

where

\[
\gamma_{L,k}=
\begin{cases}
2,&k=2,\\[2mm]
\displaystyle{\sqrt2(13L-9)\over8L},&k=3,\\[3mm]
\displaystyle\sqrt2\left(
{2k-3\over2}-{(k-2)^2\over L}\right),&k\ge4.
\end{cases}                                         \tag{4}
\]

The residual is supported on

\[
(j,m+j),\qquad0\le j\le k-2,
\]

and its transpose.  For `k=2`, `R_(L,2)=0`.  For `k=3`, put

\[
A={5L-9\over8L};
\]

the two upper-triangular entries are
`(-sqrt(2)A,2sqrt(2)A)`.  For `k>=4`, put

\[
A={2k-5\over2}-{(k-2)^2\over L},\qquad
C=2-{2(k-2)\over L}.                                \tag{5}
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
(R_{L,k})_{0,m}&=-\sqrt2A,\\
(R_{L,k})_{j,m+j}&=(2j-(k-2))C\quad(1\le j\le k-3),\\
(R_{L,k})_{k-2,L}&=2\sqrt2A.                        \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]

### Proof

The first inverse-Riemann and Stein recurrences preserve L65 circle
grade.  Mode (1) can meet only defect coordinate `e_m`.  Solving the
finite Stein equation

\[
P_1-C_p^*P_1C_p
=T_1^*P_0C_p+C_p^*P_0T_1
+e_0q_1^*+q_1e_0^*
\]

entrywise along the single chain gives (3).  Its first and last
boundary equations give (4)--(5); subtracting the defect column leaves
the arithmetic interior progression (6).  Substitution back into the
Stein equation verifies every entry, so increasing `L` is not an
extrapolation.

The base condition-number derivative only reads the two endpoint
diagonal entries.  Since `R_(L,k)` is off diagonal, (3) proves that the
pure first normal response is the matching defect response.

For `k=1`, (1) gives `m=L+1`, outside defect coordinates
`e_1,...,e_L`.  The missing column leaves L160's nonzero boundary
term, explaining why grade one is exceptional at first order.

## 3. Why this does not yet prove (2)

Use the physical path

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_\epsilon&=H_0+\epsilon H_k,\\
K_\epsilon&=H_\epsilon+R^*H_\epsilon R,\\
X_\epsilon&=2K_\epsilon^{-1/2}H_\epsilon R
                 K_\epsilon^{-1/2},\\
A_{\epsilon,s}&=X_\epsilon+\epsilon X_\epsilon^*
                 +s\epsilon^dN_m.                  \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]

After inverse Riemann pullback, differentiating in `s` produces not
only the first column at weight `d`, but mixed strong coefficients at
every weight `d+1,...,2d`.  The Stein and simple-endpoint recurrences
propagate all of them.

Leave the final defect jet free,

\[
q=e_0+\sum_{j<d}\epsilon^jq_j
 +\epsilon^d\sum_{\nu=1}^Lx_\nu e_\nu+O(\epsilon^{d+1}),
\]

and let `F_(2d)(s,x)` be the weight-`2d` condition coefficient.
The sharpened candidate, strongly suggested by the full recurrences,
is

\[
\boxed{
\left.\partial_sF_{2d}(s,x)\right|_{s=0}
=\gamma_{L,k}\partial_{x_m}F_{2d}(0,x).
}                                                     \tag{8}
\]

The coefficient is exactly the same `gamma_(L,k)` as in (4).  A
floating full-series audit verifies (8), not merely the final zero,
through grades two to five over multiple central, resonant, and
separated lengths.  In every nondegenerate case the observed
normal/defect derivative ratio agrees with (4) to floating error.

Since the defect Hessian is positive definite, successive stationarity
makes the right side of (8) zero.  Thus (8) would prove (2)
immediately.  The task is to prove the full covariant identity, not to
rediscover its coefficient.

Those mixed coefficients cannot be silently discarded.  In the exact grade-three
collision, the individual endpoint derivatives are nonzero even
though their condition-ratio combination cancels.  Thus the earlier
argument that applied a reversal functional only to (3) skipped real
terms.

There is an exact obstruction already in the ordinary L65 Hessian.
For grade three put `m=L-1`.  Away from the sole terminal resonance
`L=10`, the prepared compact matrix jet and `N_m` have length-one
reduced mode coordinates

\[
v_{\rm flat}=4(-2+\sqrt2),\qquad v_N={9\over2}.
\]

L65's scalar mode kernel is

\[
K_m={m(m-1)(m-2)\over6(m+1)^2}.
\]

Their quadratic cross coefficient is therefore

\[
\boxed{
-2v_{\rm flat}K_mv_N
=6{m(m-1)(m-2)\over(m+1)^2}(2-\sqrt2)>0.
}                                                     \tag{9}
\]

For the first central case this is `20-10sqrt(2)`.  At `L=10`, the
prepared coordinate folds to `-4` and the positive cross is `756/25`.
The complete physical cross nevertheless vanishes in the finite
checks.  Hence the higher mixed jets cancel a genuinely nonzero
base-Hessian term; the desired theorem cannot be reduced to an
orthogonality assertion at `C_p`.

The required all-size completion is a covariant differentiated
recurrence proving

\[
\boxed{
\dot\lambda_+^{(2d)}=4\dot\lambda_-^{(2d)}
}                                                     \tag{10}
\]

for the difference of the normal and
`gamma_(L,k)`-times-defect variations.  A valid proof must display the
telescoping of the complete mixed recurrence, or an adjoint identity
implying (8), rather than infer it from finite grades.

L169 now supplies the natural all-order object for such an adjoint
identity:

\[
\theta(\zeta)
=d+\zeta\sqrt\alpha\,q^T(I-\zeta T)^{-1}Jq,
\]

which is inner for the complete optimized branch.  A115's moving
constant characteristic term is precisely the feedthrough `d`.
Consequently the remaining work should prove that the marked
one-reflection functional is the real mean of the relevant
coefficient of `dot(theta)/theta`; it should not expand the
characteristic polynomial while omitting `d`.
