# Direct endpoint residue selection (L172, 2026-07-23)

## 1. Theorem

Use L171's notation

\[
2\le k\le\lfloor L/2\rfloor,\qquad
d=k+1,\qquad m=L+2-k.
\]

The direct endpoint/cofactor functional left open in L171 vanishes:

\[
\boxed{
[\epsilon^{2d}]\partial_{w_k}\partial_s R=0.
}                                                     \tag{1}
\]

Together with L171's zero-reflection and sparse singular-Hessian
reductions, this proves L163 for every grade `k>=2`.

## 2. The functional is an explicit residue

At the Crabb point put

\[
B_0(z)=z^L,\qquad
F_0(z)=e_0^*(zI-C)^{-1}e_L=2z^{-(L+1)}.
\]

For a scalar rational function `b`, Cauchy's functional calculus gives

\[
e_0^*b(T)e_L
=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb T}
 b(z)e_0^*(zI-T)^{-1}e_L\,dz.                       \tag{2}
\]

L156 derives L149's one-reflection row by applying this finite
coefficient functional to the endpoint-resolvent residual

\[
{\cal R}(z)=F_{\rm raw}(z)-\Theta(1/z).              \tag{3}
\]

At the equality point `R=0`.  Hence differentiating the functional in
the strong variable creates no derivative-of-functional term: the
direct `w_k s` coefficient is, up to a harmless nonzero real
normalization,

\[
\boxed{
[z^{-1}]\,\dot B_{w_k}(z)\,\dot{\cal R}_s(z).
}                                                     \tag{4}
\]

Equation (4) includes numerator preparation, reversed denominator,
operator realization, and endpoint-basis motion: those are precisely
the ingredients already absorbed into L156's residual formula.  It
is not an informal Hardy projection.

More explicitly, differentiating (2) before using L156 produces both
`dot(B) dot(R)` and the term in which the reflected operator changes
the resolvent applied to `dot(R)`.  L149 equation (7) defines its
`dot(Theta)` as the **total** reflected tangent and proves that these
pieces, together with the prepared denominator and outer-coordinate
conjugacy, combine into the single multiplier in (4).  Thus
`dot(B)_(w_k)` in (4) denotes that total L149 symbol, not merely the
scalar-numerator derivative.  This is why no `B_0 R_(ws)` term has
been dropped.

## 3. The two factors and their powers

L140's monomial-inner calculation, inserted in L149's total tangent,
gives the complete first reflected Blaschke symbol:

\[
{\dot B_{w_k}\over B_0}
=a z^{-k}-\overline a z^k .
\]

Thus its only scalar powers are

\[
\boxed{L-k,\quad L+k.}                               \tag{5}
\]

L168 gives the raw strong endpoint response, while L169--L170 give
the matching characteristic/Schur response.  Their relative
difference is

\[
(\beta_{L,k}+k)z^m.
\]

Multiplication by `F_0` converts this to the absolute residual in
(3):

\[
\dot{\cal R}_s(z)
=c_{L,k}z^{m-(L+1)}
=c_{L,k}z^{1-k},                                    \tag{6}
\]

where the scalar `c_(L,k)` and the factor two in `F_0` are irrelevant
to vanishing.

Multiplying (5) by (6), the only possible powers in (4) are

\[
\boxed{
L-2k+1,\qquad L+1.
}                                                     \tag{7}
\]

Since `k<=L/2`,

\[
L-2k+1\ge1,\qquad L+1\ge1.                          \tag{8}
\]

Neither power is `-1`, so the Cauchy residue in (4) is zero.  This
proves (1).  The central fold `L=2k` is included: its first power is
exactly one.

Complex phases only conjugate the two coefficients in (5), not their
powers, so the same proof covers both real polarizations.

## 4. Grade one is not erased

The proof starts at `k=2`.  At `k=1`, L167 gives a nonzero constant
characteristic variation and L170 gives `dot B=0`; moreover L171's
two sparse coupling indices no longer exist as the disjoint
`L-k`/`k-2` pair.  The ordinary endpoint/feedthrough face therefore
survives and is exactly the nonzero L160/L165 coefficient.  No step
above asserts otherwise.
